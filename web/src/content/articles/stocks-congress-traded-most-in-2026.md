The stocks that the most members of Congress traded in 2026 are the largest companies in the market. Microsoft and Apple were each traded by 26 members, and Nvidia by 20. In eight of the ten most widely traded stocks, more members reported selling than buying.

The counts come from the periodic transaction reports published on this site with a filing date in 2026: House reports through November 12 and Senate reports through October 1. Together they hold 5,359 purchases and sales by 129 members, 100 in the House and 29 in the Senate.

## Which stocks did the most members trade?

The table ranks stocks by the number of members who reported at least one purchase or sale. A member who both bought and sold the same stock is counted in both columns.

| Stock | Members | Trades | Members who bought | Members who sold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft (MSFT) | 26 | 72 | 15 | 18 |
| Apple (AAPL) | 26 | 65 | 12 | 19 |
| Nvidia (NVDA) | 20 | 56 | 11 | 14 |
| Amazon (AMZN) | 17 | 37 | 11 | 12 |
| Alphabet Class A (GOOGL) | 16 | 37 | 7 | 12 |
| Home Depot (HD) | 15 | 40 | 6 | 12 |
| Meta Platforms (META) | 14 | 27 | 11 | 7 |
| Procter & Gamble (PG) | 13 | 29 | 4 | 10 |
| Chevron (CVX) | 13 | 19 | 7 | 8 |
| UnitedHealth Group (UNH) | 12 | 28 | 9 | 8 |

Ranking by members and not by trades keeps one very active account from deciding the order. A single member with a managed account can file dozens of trades in one stock; 26 members trading the same stock is a different kind of fact.

## Were members buying or selling?

Taken across every security, purchases outnumber sales: 2,925 against 2,434. Among the ten most widely held names it runs the other way. More members sold than bought in eight of the ten, and the gap is widest in Procter & Gamble (10 sellers, 4 buyers), Home Depot (12 and 6) and Apple (19 and 12). Meta Platforms and UnitedHealth Group are the two where buyers outnumber sellers.

The counts are of members, not dollars. A trade report gives a dollar range for each trade, such as $1,001 to $15,000, and not an exact amount, so adding up dollars means adding up ranges. The guide to [congressional trade amount ranges](/guides/congress-trade-amount-ranges/) explains what the ranges are and how far they can be trusted.

## How concentrated is the trading?

Not very. The 5,359 trades include 4,034 in a listed stock with a ticker symbol, spread over 911 different stocks. The other 1,325 are in bonds, funds and other assets. The ten stocks in the table account for 410 trades, about a tenth of the stock trades. Most of what members report is one or two members trading a stock that nobody else in Congress traded this year.

## What do the reports not show?

A periodic transaction report is a short form. It names the asset, the date, the direction and a dollar range, and it says whether the trade was in the member's own account, a spouse's, a joint account or a dependent child's. It does not give the price or the number of shares, so it cannot show a gain or a loss. It does not say who decided on the trade: many members' accounts are run by an adviser. And it does not show what the member still holds. Holdings appear only in the annual disclosure, which is a separate filing.

Two limits in the data are worth knowing as well. Senate reports reach this site in batches, so Senate trades disclosed after October 1 are not counted yet. And reports filed on paper are held back until they have been transcribed and checked.
